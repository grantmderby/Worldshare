WorldShare — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 August 2026

WorldShare is a Minecraft mod that keeps one singleplayer world in sync between two people over Google Drive. This page explains, in plain language, what it accesses, everywhere your data goes, and what the developer can and can't see.

What WorldShare accesses in your Google account

WorldShare requests a single Google Drive permission, drive.file — the narrowest scope Google offers for this purpose. It grants access only to the specific files you personally select in Google's own file picker, plus files WorldShare itself creates for you.

It cannot browse your Drive, cannot see files you didn't select, and cannot reach anything created later without you selecting that too. It requests no access to Gmail, Contacts, Photos, or any other Google service.

In practice you select a fixed set of files once, when you first set up a shared world: one small settings file, one presence file, and a number of world archives. They toggle on a single click, and the selection is remembered permanently.

What gets uploaded

Your Minecraft world's save data — region files, player data, and related world state — packed into those archives, so the other player can download and continue the same world. Alongside it, a small settings file records which files the world consists of, who currently has the world open, and the display name you chose in WorldShare's settings.

Where your data goes

Three places, and no others:

Destination What is sent When
Google Drive
(your own account)		 Your world's save data and the settings file described above. Whenever you sync a world.
Modrinth
api.modrinth.com		 SHA-1 hashes of the mod .jar files installed in your game — not the files themselves, and nothing about you or your world. When a world's mod list is published or checked, so the other player can be told which mods they need.
e4mc relay
(via the separate e4mc mod)		 Live gameplay traffic, while you and the other player are playing together at the same time. Only during a live co-op session.

WorldShare does not operate a server of its own, and world data is never routed through anything the developer controls. e4mc is a separate mod with its own operators and its own terms; live co-op uses it, and that part of your traffic is subject to their policy rather than this one.

What the developer can see

Nothing. The developer has no access to your Drive files, your world data, your Google account, or any record of you using the mod. WorldShare collects no analytics, no telemetry, and no crash reports.

What's stored on your computer

Your Google sign-in token is stored only on your own machine, in your Minecraft installation's config folder. It is sent nowhere except to Google, and is never written into your world files or anything you share.

Revoking access

You can revoke WorldShare's access at any time from Google's own settings: myaccount.google.com/permissions. Revoking does not delete anything already in your Drive — it only stops WorldShare from reading or writing those files until you sign in again.

The files themselves are ordinary files in your Drive. You can move, rename, or delete them yourself at any time, with or without the mod.

Children

WorldShare is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal information from anyone. Signing in uses a Google account, which is subject to Google's own age requirements.

Changes to this policy

If what the mod accesses ever changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top is updated. Past versions are visible in the project's public Git history.

Questions

WorldShare's source code is public — see the GitHub repository for exactly how it uses the Drive API, or open an issue there to ask.
