WorldShare

Share one Minecraft singleplayer world with someone far away, without running a dedicated server.

WorldShare is a Minecraft mod that lets two players keep a single singleplayer world in sync through Google Drive, with session locking so you never overwrite each other's progress. Either of you can open the world solo whenever you like; when you're both online at the same time, you play together live over a peer-to-peer relay.

How it works

  1. One of you sets up a world. WorldShare creates a set of files in a Drive folder you choose.
  2. You share that folder with the other player, the way you'd share any Drive folder.
  3. They sign in once and select those files. That's the whole setup — it never has to be repeated.
  4. From then on, whoever plays pushes their changes when they're done, and the other pulls them next time they play.
What it needs from you: a Google account, and permission to read and write the specific files you select — nothing else in your Drive. WorldShare uses drive.file, the narrowest Drive permission Google offers, which grants access only to files you personally pick. See the privacy policy for exactly what that means and everywhere your data goes.

What it isn't

There's no dedicated server and no third-party hosting. Your world lives in your own Google Drive, in a folder you chose, reachable only by the people you shared it with. The developer has no access to any of it, and the mod collects no analytics.

Live co-op runs through e4mc, a separate mod you'll also need installed. It's only used while you're actually playing together.
